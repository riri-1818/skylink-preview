Working Holiday

Working holiday:
what you can do, what it costs.

A visa that lets you combine study, work and travel over a year, your way. To make it a year that leads somewhere after you go home, start with the conditions and a rough budget.

On a Working Holiday visa you can

  • Stay for up to 12 months
  • Do any kind of work (usually up to 6 months with one employer)
  • Study for up to 4 months (17 weeks)
  • Leave and re-enter Australia as many times as you like
  • Become eligible for a second-year visa after 3 months of specified work

Working holiday FAQ

Is there an age limit?

You must be 18 to 30 when you apply, so your online application needs to be lodged before your 31st birthday.

Can I go if I don't speak much English?

The visa has no English requirement, but your English makes a big difference to the jobs you can get. Many people spend their first few months at language school and prepare an English résumé before job hunting. In the consultation we plan your preparation with you.

Do I need insurance?

It is not a visa condition, but the Australian Government strongly recommends it, because you pay all your own medical costs in Australia. We can go through the options with you.

When should I apply?

The Department of Home Affairs expects processing to take around three months on average, and advises not to book flights or commit to work or study until the visa is granted. Work back from when you want to leave and start early.

Can I stay a second year?

If you do 3 months of specified work in your first year, you become eligible to apply for a second Working Holiday visa. The eligible work and areas are defined, so we check the latest rules with you.

What about my resident registration and pension in Japan?

The paperwork at your local office before you leave, such as the notice of moving overseas, depends on how long you stay and your situation. In the consultation we list what you need to do before departure.

FAQ.

Frequently asked questions

Can I study in Australia as a beginner in English?

Yes. Language schools test your level on entry and have beginner classes. Preparation before you leave makes a real difference, so in your consultation we suggest schools for your level and how to study until departure.

How much does it cost?

It varies widely with length, city, school and accommodation. The main costs are tuition, accommodation, flights, insurance, visa fees and living expenses. We give you an estimate based on your plans in your consultation.

Student visa or Working Holiday visa?

A student visa suits those who want to study seriously or plan vocational or further study. A Working Holiday visa suits those who put work experience and flexibility first (age and other conditions apply). For up to three months, short-term study on a visitor visa is also an option.

Can I work while studying?

Student visas have a limit on working hours. Working Holiday visas allow work, with conditions such as how long you can work for one employer. You cannot work on a visitor visa. Conditions change, so we check the latest with you.

Will my English improve on a short stay?

With less time, a clear goal matters more — getting used to speaking, or trying out a city before a longer stay. We also share ways to spend more of your stay using English.
